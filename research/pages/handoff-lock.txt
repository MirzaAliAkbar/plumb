 Handoff Lock
 Approve the report, not the filename. Handoff Lock blocks CSV exports when the contents or recipient change after approval, then helps prepare a clean copy for review.
 Like
 Comment
 Story 
 Updates 
 Submission history 
 Inspiration
 A donation coordinator approves a CSV confirming that 12 laptops arrived. The report is regenerated before it is shared. Its filename stays the same, but it now includes recipient phone numbers and device serial numbers. That is the mistake Handoff Lock is designed to catch before the file leaves the coordinator’s hands.
 What it does
 Handoff Lock gives coordinators a final check before exporting a donor report. It ties approval to the recipient, filename, and exact contents of the CSV. If any of them changes, the previous approval cannot release the new version. The app explains what changed and, when extra columns are the problem, can prepare a donor copy with only the permitted fields. That copy needs its own approval.
 How I built and tested it
 The prototype was developed for LaunchPad Hacks using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. It runs in the browser and checks the report against an explicit list of permitted fields. On export, it compares the file against the approved snapshot byte for byte and records the decision in a session log.
 The key design challenge was making a blocked export actionable: the coordinator needs to know what changed and how to fix it. I tested the complete flow in Safari. Handoff Lock blocked the altered file, removed the two extra columns from the donor copy, and exported the copy after I approved it. The downloaded CSV still showed 12 laptops and contained neither of the extra columns.
 Try it yourself
 Download and unzip the build, then open index.html. Click Approve this version, select Same filename, private fields, and click Run gate & download. After the block, click Prepare donor copy, approve the new version, and run the gate again.
 The demo uses fictional data. It controls downloads from its own page and does not send email or verify delivery. I used OpenAI Codex to help develop and test the prototype and draft the submission text.
