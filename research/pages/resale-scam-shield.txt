 Runway Check
 Enter your pay, bills and savings and see week by week whether your money lasts through retraining after an AI layoff.
 Like
 Comment
 Story 
 Updates 
 Submission history 
 Inspiration
 When someone loses a job to automation, the first advice is almost always "go retrain." Nobody says how you're supposed to pay rent during the months that takes. Savings run down, unemployment pays a fraction of what you made, and rent is due either way. I wanted one person to be able to see that math for their own life.
 What it does
 You put in your pay, hours, rent, health coverage, other bills, savings, your weekly unemployment benefit and how long the training lasts. Runway Check plays it out week by week and shows the week your money runs out and how far short you'd be. With the example numbers, a 24 week program runs dry in week 9 and you finish about $2,654 short, which is $111 a week of extra income you'd need.
 Below the calculator there are four tabs.
 Close the gap lets you tap changes you could really make, like cutting rent by $300, earning $100 a week on the side or paying less for health coverage. They stack, so you can see if a few together close the gap.
 Worth it? takes a program cost and the new job's pay and shows how long it takes to come out ahead. A $3,000 program and a $24 an hour job leave you $7,246 in the hole on day one, but you're back above zero in about 22 weeks.
 First week is a checklist for the days right after a layoff, with links to find your state's unemployment benefit.
 Save and share lets you save scenarios, copy a share link, download a spreadsheet or add the key dates to your calendar.
 How I built it
 It's one HTML file with plain CSS and JavaScript, hosted on GitHub Pages. There are no accounts and no server, so nothing you type leaves your browser. I checked the math by hand on a few cases.
 The research behind it
 The tool grew out of research I've done on wages and living costs from 2000 to 2024, written up in two papers, "The Widening Gap" and "The Safety Net Was Not Built for This." That work is why the site can show your rent in 2000 dollars, and both papers are linked at the bottom of the page. The papers were finished before this event. Runway Check itself was built for LaunchPad Hacks.
 Limits
 It's a simple weekly estimate. It leaves out taxes, severance, a partner's income and job search costs. The $450 a week for 26 weeks benefit is a placeholder, so put in your own state's numbers. It isn't financial advice.
